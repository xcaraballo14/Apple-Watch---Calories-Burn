DATA USE & COMPLIANCE

Document Version 2.0  ·  Updated July 22, 2026

A technical and regulatory reference for the data BurnReward accesses, how the solo game keeps it on-device, and how the optional social features share the data you choose.

0. TWO MODES — READ THIS FIRST

BurnReward operates in two modes. Solo mode (the default, no account) keeps everything on your device exactly as documented in Sections 3–4: your quests, level, and records are computed from Apple Health on the iPhone and never leave it. Social mode (optional, the "Guild") begins only when you sign in and consent; it shares the game data you choose — including real metrics like calories and heart rate — with the party members you accept, stored on our backend (Supabase). Sections describing "no server / nothing leaves the device" apply to solo mode; the backend and what's shared are documented in Section 3.5.

2. HEALTHKIT DATA TYPES — COMPLETE INVENTORY

BurnReward uses the following HealthKit types across the Apple Watch app (which records the workout) and the iPhone app (which reads the saved workouts back). This is the complete and exhaustive list — no data type is accessed without declaration.

Data Type HKType Identifier Permission Purpose Retention
Active Energy Burned HKQuantityType(.activeEnergyBurned) READ
Watch (live) · iPhone		 The Watch reads the live kilocalorie sum from HKLiveWorkoutBuilder during a quest, comparing it to the reward's calorie goal to fill the EXP bar and fire milestone haptics. The iPhone reads the calories stored on each saved BurnReward workout to build quest history, XP, and records. In-memory during the workout; the Watch keeps the current quest's value in UserDefaults until a new quest starts. The iPhone recomputes from HealthKit on every refresh and stores no copy.
Heart Rate HKQuantityType(.heartRate) READ
Watch (live) · iPhone		 The Watch shows live BPM and computes the workout average for the summary. The iPhone reads that average from each saved workout for the quest summary/receipt and the XP intensity factor. In-memory during the workout; the Watch keeps the summary average in UserDefaults until a new quest. The iPhone recomputes from HealthKit, no copy.
Step Count HKQuantityType(.stepCount) READ
Watch (live) · iPhone		 The Watch shows live steps during a quest. The iPhone reads the step total from each saved workout for the quest detail and the "most steps" personal record. In-memory during the workout; the iPhone recomputes from HealthKit on refresh, no copy.
Workout (HKWorkout) HKWorkoutType.workoutType() WRITE Watch
READ iPhone		 WRITE (Watch): when the quest's goals are met, the HKWorkoutSession is finalized and the HKWorkout is saved to Apple Health via finishWorkout(), stamped with the reward's name, emoji, and calorie goal as workout metadata, so it counts toward the user's rings. READ (iPhone): the app queries the workouts BurnReward itself saved (matched by app source) and reconstructs the entire quest history, level, badges, records, and weekly challenge from them — nothing is stored separately. Once written, the workout is owned and managed by Apple Health. BurnReward keeps no copy; the iPhone reads it fresh on each refresh.

HealthKit Privacy Note: Apple's HealthKit intentionally hides read-authorization status from apps (a privacy design so an app can't infer which categories a user has blocked). BurnReward therefore never drives its UI from HealthKit's read-status APIs — it relies only on real operations (a save succeeding, samples arriving). If a quest collects no sensor data, the Watch shows a one-time "no sensor data" hint pointing to iPhone Settings › Privacy & Security › Health › BurnReward. The iPhone reads only the workouts BurnReward itself saved (matched by app source) — never any other app's health data.

3. ON-DEVICE STORAGE — COMPLETE INVENTORY

BurnReward stores data exclusively on the user's own devices — never on a server. The Apple Watch uses UserDefaults (standard on-device key-value store) plus an App Group container shared with the watch-face complication; the iPhone uses its own on-device UserDefaults. Nothing here is transmitted off the device.

3C. iPHONE — USERDEFAULTS (STANDARD)

The iPhone app derives everything it shows from the workouts already saved in Apple Health, so it stores no health data of its own. Its UserDefaults holds only the profile fields the user chooses, the user's reward library, notification preferences, and small "already handled" markers so celebrations and prompts fire once.

Local notifications: The iPhone schedules badge, level-up, record, streak, and weekly-challenge reminders with UNUserNotificationCenter. They are composed and delivered entirely on-device — there is no push server, no device token, and nothing is transmitted.

Key point (solo mode): Everything above stays on the device. It is numeric workout metrics, reward names from the user's own list, timing data, and app preferences, plus an optional local display name and photo. In solo mode there is no account and none of it leaves the device. What social mode adds is documented next.

3.5. SOCIAL MODE — BACKEND DATA (WHAT YOU SHARE)

The social features are optional and off by default. They begin only after the user signs in with Apple and agrees to an in-app consent screen that discloses exactly what is shared. In social mode, the data the user chooses to share is stored on our backend, provided by Supabase (a hosted Postgres database, authentication, and object storage). Everything is still derived on the device first; only the chosen summaries are uploaded. Raw HealthKit samples are never uploaded.

Stored on BackendContainsWho Can See ItContains Health Metrics?
Account Sign in with Apple identifier; a chosen username. Optional Apple relay email (forwards without revealing the real address). Account holder; username is public to signed-in players ✗ No
Public profile Username, level, title, earned badge names Any signed-in player (enables friend search) ✗ No
Shared character Level, class, XP, streaks, trophies, and records — including calories, average heart rate, and step counts Accepted party only (row-level security), and only while "Show my character" is on YES
Feed posts & photos Quest/badge/level-up summaries (may include a workout's calories and duration), an optional caption, and up to three photos (EXIF-stripped, on-device content-screened) Accepted party only YES (summary metrics)
Reactions Which reaction the user left on a party member's post Accepted party only ✗ No
Weekly score The user's XP total for the week (only if they opt into the challenge) Accepted party only ✗ No (XP is a game score, not a raw metric)
Social graph & safety Friend requests / accepted friends, blocks, and reports the user files Enforced by row-level security; reports are reviewed by the developer ✗ No

Access boundary: The app ships only a restricted publishable key; it cannot bypass the database's row-level security. Those rules — not the client — decide what each signed-in player can read, so a user's shared character and photos are readable only by the user and the party members they have accepted. Blocking a player severs this in both directions.

Control & deletion: Every share is reversible in Settings (make the character private, leave the challenge, delete a post, block a player), which removes the shared copy. Deleting the account removes the profile, shared character, posts, photos, reactions, scores, and social-graph records from the backend.

5. APPLE APP STORE COMPLIANCE

APP STORE CONNECT — DATA NUTRITION LABEL

Apple's "App Privacy" questionnaire treats data as collected when it is transmitted off the device beyond servicing the immediate request. In solo mode, nothing meets that definition — HealthKit is read and used entirely on-device. In social mode, the data the user chooses to share is transmitted to our backend, so it must be declared as collected and Linked to You. Nothing is used for Tracking (we never share data with brokers or use it for cross-app/site advertising). The accurate label is below.

How to fill in App Store Connect: For each collected type above, select the type → mark it used for App Functionality (do not check Third-Party Advertising, Developer's Advertising, Analytics, or Product Personalization) → mark it Linked to the user's identity → answer No to "used for tracking." Health & Fitness lives under the "Health & Fitness" data type; photos and captions under "User Content"; the account id/username under "Identifiers"; the Apple relay email under "Contact Info → Email Address." Because sharing is opt-in and off by default, this is the label once a user turns social on — but Apple requires declaring any data the app is capable of collecting, so it must be set now.

COPPA (Children's Online Privacy Protection Act)

BurnReward is not directed to children under 13, and the Terms of Service set a minimum age of 13. Solo mode collects no personal information. Social mode involves creating an account and sharing information with other users, so it is intended for users 13 and older; if we learn that we have collected account information from a child under 13, we will delete it. The social consent screen and age gate exist to prevent under-13 accounts.

HIPAA (Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act)

HIPAA applies to covered entities (healthcare providers, insurers) and their business associates. BurnReward is a consumer fitness app, not a healthcare provider, and does not create, receive, maintain, or transmit Protected Health Information (PHI) as defined by HIPAA. The app does not interface with any healthcare provider system and is not a medical device. HIPAA does not apply.
